## The game

### "You're helping the world see what's really happening down there"

You are a researcher diving into a reef zone during the **Fourth Global Coral Bleaching Event**. You have read the reports, the satellite images and the thermal stress models; this is the first time you see it with your own eyes.

Your field notebook, the **Reef Observation Log**, holds nine photos of the reef as it used to be: bright, crowded, alive. Each one is greyed out until you find the same spot today and photograph it as it is now, pale and bleached. Between the photos, the game hands you two other points of view: a **coral** trying to feed itself, and a **clownfish** running from predators.

The ending leaves the game world for the real one: the data behind the bleaching event, footage of reef restoration work, and conservation programs a player can join.

## My contribution

As **gameplay programmer and technical director**, I built the systems the player touches and brought the team's work together into one build:

- **Diver controller:** third-person swimming with sprint (Shift), ascend (E) and descend (Q), a flashlight (F) and a first/third-person switch (T).
- **Camera mode:** a first-person camera with a viewfinder HUD. Near one of the nine spots a crosshair appears and turns green when the shot lines up; a correct photo fills that page of the log and opens it.
- **Reef Observation Log:** the 0/9 gallery of then-and-now photos, with each shot's date and time and a progress counter.
- **Clownfish chase:** a quick-time escape on spline paths. I computed the fish and predator paths with flexible meeting points and branching routes; predators strike from either side and you dodge with A or D. Getting caught leads to a game-over screen and a retry from the start of the chase.
- **Coral feeding:** the side story where you play a coral catching plankton.
- **Tutorial guide:** instead of on-screen instructions, a clownfish swims ahead on a path that teaches movement, sprint, the view switch, ascend and descend, the flashlight and the camera.
- **Integration and packaging:** on 19 March 2026 I merged every part (cinematics, UI, gallery, photo spots, reef collisions, conclusion) into one game and ran it end to end, ready for packaging.

## Level design

The reef has two layers. The **tutorial layer** teaches movement and the camera on a single path, with a dead end and the first photo spot. The **middle layer** is the open reef: a critical path and side paths between coral walls, predator patrols, anemones as safe spots, tentacle blockers that open only at night, and the rest of the photo spots, numbered 1 to 9 on the map.

## How it came together

- **November 2025:** the team settled on the reef ecosystem, then reworked the gameplay around exploration: gathering evidence that the reef is dying, with a coral and a clownfish side quest.
- **December 2025:** the narrative was finalized; the coral became a narrative scene and the clownfish a quick-time chase on spline paths.
- **January 2026:** I built the diver's movement, then the camera mode and the photo gallery, then the clownfish chase. The game got its name.
- **February 2026:** the diver and the chase went into the real map, the tutorial switched to a clownfish guide, and I built the coral gameplay and the game-over and retry flow.
- **March 2026:** cinematics, UI and the conclusion landed; I integrated everything, built the tutorial guide path, and the team packaged and tested the game.

## Responsible AI use

The Games for Change rules ask teams to report their use of AI. The narration voices in iWasThere are AI-generated; the script was written by the team.
