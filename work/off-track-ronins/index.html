My focus

Performance capture, retargeting, Control Rig, Sequencer and lighting, volumetric capture and ICVFX.

Production log: forging the scene

Week 1: Environment and atmosphere

The visual direction relied on a sharp contrast. We built a sterile, monochrome office designed to feel suffocating and repetitive, with rigid grid layouts that stress the protagonist’s entrapment.

The samurai world, in contrast, is a vibrant, sun-scorched post-apocalyptic wasteland. We worked from an “asset zoo”, curating high-fidelity props so the cut from the dull corporate world to the chaotic battlefield feels jarring and powerful.

Focus: environment design · Unreal Engine · art direction

Week 2: Combat choreography

We moved from the desk to the dojo. This week was all combat choreography, translating the protagonist’s inner rage into decisive samurai movement. We broke the “crash out” sequence down beat by beat, practicing with prop katanas for safety and fluidity.

The focus was on performance: making the weight behind each strike feel real before bringing it onto the motion capture stage. We spent hours refining the timing of the parries and strikes to match the fast rhythm of the final edit.

Focus: fight choreography · stunt coordination · mocap preparation

Week 3: Retargeting and the Live Link pipeline

To prepare for the motion capture shoot, I built a retargeting pipeline that maps the OptiTrack skeleton onto three different targets: the standard UE5 Manny, a high-fidelity MetaHuman and our custom Samurai model.

The Samurai was the hard one: its non-standard bone names broke auto-generation, so I chained the IK Rigs by hand to make it work with the IK Retargeter.

To stream data from Motive to Unreal without friction, I built a modular workflow: IK Rigs → IK Retargeter → Animation Blueprint → Actor Blueprint. The same setup takes any major skeleton type.

Focus: IK retargeting · Motive Live Link · Animation Blueprints

Week 4: Real-time performance capture

Production peaked on the motion capture stage, with a full real-time previsualization workflow. Motive streamed straight into UE5, tracking the actors’ skeletons and the rigid-body props at the same time.

This live feedback let us see the “crash out” in real time and make sure the stunt performances lined up with the virtual environment. The session was a marathon of technical precision, recorded in our behind-the-scenes timelapse and team photos.

Focus: real-time previz · rigid-body tracking · stunt performance

Week 5: Animation cleanup and sequencing

Post-production started with selecting the mocap takes and processing them in MotionBuilder. I handled dual-skeleton retargeting and rigid-body integration so the physical props stay in sync with the character animation.

For the precision a sword fight needs, I used an IK/FK Control Rig workflow on the MetaHuman skeleton. It let me refine scenes 01 to 29 frame by frame for exact blade contact and grounded footwork that raw mocap often misses.

Finally, I assembled a Level Sequencer for each scene, set up the cinematic cameras and locked the shot direction that drives the pacing.

Focus: MotionBuilder · Control Rig cleanup · Sequencer assembly

Week 6: ICVFX and volumetric integration

To capture the protagonist’s suffocating reality before the transition, we shot on an LED volume (ICVFX) with real-time camera tracking. That gave the office scenes correct parallax and lighting in camera.

For the pivotal “crash out”, when the worker throws paper in rage, we used a volumetric capture workflow. It allowed a seamless transition from the live-action footage into the CGI samurai world, blurring the line between physical and digital.

The project finished in DaVinci Resolve, where my team handled the final compositing: merging the SFX, VFX and CGI with the live-action footage, and locking the grade that sharpens the contrast between the sterile office and the vivid wasteland.

Focus: LED volume / ICVFX · volumetric capture · DaVinci Resolve
