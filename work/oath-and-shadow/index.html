My focus

Mocap cleaning, retargeting, Sequencer and lighting: taking a two-actor sword fight from the OptiTrack stage, through MotionBuilder, to the final render in Unreal Engine 5.

Production log: the 0 to 1 process

Week 1: Motion capture and data strategy

We used the OptiTrack system to record complex sword-fighting choreography. The main challenge was managing occlusion during close-combat sequences.

After capture, I brought the raw data into MotionBuilder. The raw fight capture came without a mesh, so I inspected the take in MotionBuilder first and retargeted it to a Mixamo character to verify the bone rotations before export.

Focus: OptiTrack · MotionBuilder setup · raw data cleaning

Week 2: MetaHuman and Live Link

I set up the Live Link connection to stream animation data directly from MotionBuilder to Unreal Engine 5. This allowed real-time visualization of the performance on our target assets.

Problem solving: a retargeting error twisted the MetaHuman’s forearms unnaturally. It came from a mismatch in the T-pose definition between the source data and the MetaHuman skeleton, which I resolved by adjusting the bone mapping in the retargeting settings.

Focus: Live Link · MetaHuman retargeting · real-time visualization

Week 3: Multi-character Blueprinting

We chose a medieval cathedral environment in the style of Assassin’s Creed Unity. I created a modular Blueprint system where the Knight and the Assassin could share a single animation skeleton.

This was crucial because our raw mocap file held the data for both actors at once. By mapping two character Blueprints to their own data streams, I could drive the entire fight scene from a single source file and keep the two performers perfectly in sync.

Focus: character Blueprints · shared skeletons · environment staging

Week 4: Control Rig and Sequencer

The final stage was heavy cleanup with Control Rig to fix jitter and foot sliding. The raw data often ignored the physical weapons, so I constrained the sword assets to the hand bones by hand and cleaned up the intersection points.

In the Level Sequencer, I keyed camera moves that follow the action, adjusting focal lengths to emphasize the intensity of the combat, before sending the shots to the Movie Render Queue.

Focus: Control Rig cleanup · weapon constraints · camera sequencing
